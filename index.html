Rinual Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Rinual is an iPhone app for keeping track of subscriptions, bills, renewals and expirations, and payment plans. It is made by an independent developer, called “the developer” below. This policy explains what happens to the information you enter in Rinual and what Rinual sends over the internet. It covers the current version of the app, including its TestFlight beta.

In short

  • What you enter is stored on your iPhone. Rinual has no account and no server of its own.
  • iCloud sync is optional and stays off until you turn it on. When it’s on, your data is kept in your own private iCloud database, which the developer can’t read.
  • Rinual contains no advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code, and no third-party code.
  • Rinual connects only to Apple’s services: iCloud when sync is on, the App Store for the optional tip, and the App Store’s search when you ask for an app icon.
  • Apart from what Apple shares with the developer under its own terms (see “Diagnostics” and “TestFlight beta”), the developer receives information from you only if you choose to send it, such as an email or TestFlight feedback.

What Rinual stores on your iPhone

Everything you enter is stored on your iPhone, in Rinual’s own app storage:

  • your subscriptions, bills, renewals and payment plans (“items” below): their names, amounts and currencies, dates and schedules, categories, billing sources, card colours, notes and links;
  • each item’s payment history, amount changes and other history;
  • each item’s reminder settings;
  • a subscription’s icon, if you choose a photo or an App Store icon for it.

Rinual also stores its settings and a few small app states on your iPhone: appearance, payday, default reminder time, the App Store country or region you last chose, whether you finished the welcome screen, whether you answered “Not Now” when Rinual explained reminders, which ended free trials you’ve already been told about, category renames waiting to reach iCloud, and, if you use iCloud sync, the sync status described below.

A billing source is a short name you type, such as “Apple Pay”. Rinual never asks for card numbers, bank details or passwords, and it refuses a billing-source name that looks like a card number.

For its widgets, Rinual keeps a small summary in a storage area on your iPhone that only Rinual and its widgets use (see “Widgets”).

Rinual also keeps small internal records on your iPhone, for example about a deletion that hasn’t finished. They hold internal identifiers, dates and status codes, not names or amounts.

Backups. Rinual doesn’t exclude its data from iPhone backups. If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, iOS includes Rinual’s data, as it does for other apps. This is separate from iCloud sync and happens whether or not sync is on.

No account

Rinual has no account of its own and never asks you to sign in. iCloud sync uses the Apple Account already signed in on your iPhone. Rinual doesn’t ask for your name, email address, phone number, postal address, age, contacts or location.

iCloud sync

iCloud sync is optional. It stays off until you turn on Sync with iCloud in Rinual’s Settings. While it’s off, Rinual sends nothing to iCloud and doesn’t check your iCloud account.

When you turn it on:

  • Rinual keeps your information in your own private iCloud database, so it appears on your other iPhones (or iPads running Rinual) that are signed in to the same Apple Account and have sync turned on. It counts toward your iCloud storage.
  • Everything you enter syncs: your items and their history, payments, reminder settings, categories, billing sources and icons.
  • Three settings also sync, through iCloud key-value storage: the default reminder time, your payday, and which ended free trials you’ve already been told about (kept as internal identifiers and dates, without names).
  • Your appearance choice, your App Store country or region, your notification permission, and the notifications scheduled on each iPhone, including Remind Me Later, stay on that iPhone.

Your private iCloud database belongs to your Apple Account. The developer can’t read it. Rinual has no server of its own, doesn’t use iCloud’s public or shared databases, and doesn’t share your records with anyone.

While sync is on, Rinual checks which iCloud account is signed in when it opens, each time you return to it, and when iOS reports an account change.

How synced data is protected

Rinual asks iCloud to store these as encrypted fields:

  • the names you give your items, categories and billing sources;
  • a payment-plan provider name you type under Other;
  • if you picked an item from Rinual’s list of services, which service it is (for example, “netflix”), and if you picked a payment-plan provider from the list, which one (Tabby, Tamara or Madfu);
  • notes and links;
  • every amount, including payments and the amount changes in an item’s history;
  • icon images.

Apple says iCloud encrypts encrypted fields on your device before saving them to iCloud. If you turn on Advanced Data Protection for iCloud, Apple says these fields are end-to-end encrypted, so only your trusted devices can read them. With standard data protection, Apple keeps the keys in its data centers, as it does for most iCloud data.

Everything else is stored in your private database as ordinary fields. They have iCloud’s standard protection (Apple says app data in iCloud is encrypted in transit and on its servers), but they aren’t encrypted fields. They include:

  • the kind of each item (subscription, bill, renewal or payment plan), its status (for example, active or archived), its currency codes, and whether a bill’s amount is fixed or varies;
  • schedules and dates: how often an item repeats and the calendar it uses, its start, due and paid dates, whether it’s a free trial and when the trial ends, the number of installments, and the state of each payment;
  • each item’s card colour, and whether its icon is a photo or an App Store icon;
  • reminder settings (on or off, how many days before, and the time);
  • the types and dates of history events;
  • an icon’s image format and size;
  • internal record identifiers, the links between records (for example, which category an item belongs to), and when records were created, updated, archived, deleted or renamed;
  • after you use Upload This iPhone’s Data, an upload mark on each item, category and billing source: the time of the upload and a random identifier.

The three synced settings are kept in iCloud key-value storage, not in your private database. They have iCloud’s standard protection, but they aren’t encrypted fields.

iCloud account changes

  • To know which account it syncs with, Rinual keeps, on your iPhone only, an identifier that iCloud gives Rinual for your account. It isn’t your name or email address, and Rinual doesn’t send it anywhere.
  • If you sign out of iCloud, sync pauses and your information stays on your iPhone. Sync resumes by itself when you sign in again with the same account.
  • If a different Apple Account signs in, sync pauses and Rinual tells you. Nothing is uploaded to the new account unless you choose Use This iPhone’s Data with This Account.
  • When the account changes while Rinual is syncing, iOS can remove the synced data from your iPhone. To guard against this, Rinual keeps a full copy of your data, icons included, on your iPhone while sync is working. If iOS removes the data, Rinual restores the copy, turns sync off and tells you.
  • If your data is removed through iCloud and Rinual can’t tell whether that was intended, for example because it was deleted on another device, Rinual keeps the copy apart on this iPhone and asks whether to Restore the Copy or Delete the Copy. The copy stays until you decide, until Rinual learns the data was deleted from all devices, or until you use Delete All Data.
  • Upload This iPhone’s Data and Use This iPhone’s Data with This Account upload a copy of this iPhone’s data to the account signed in now. They don’t remove anything from the previous account’s iCloud. Use iCloud’s Copy replaces the data on this iPhone with what iCloud has.

Turning sync off

Turning sync off keeps your data on this iPhone and stops syncing it and the three settings. What’s already in iCloud and on your other iPhones stays there; to delete it, see “Delete All Data”. Rinual removes its sync copy from this iPhone, and keeps the account identifier so that, if you turn sync on again, it can tell whether it’s the same account.

App Store icons

You can give a subscription its app’s icon from the App Store. Rinual asks Apple’s iTunes Search service (itunes.apple.com) only after you tap Use App Store Icon or Choose Another App:

  • For a service you picked from Rinual’s list, the request is sent as soon as the icon screen opens. It contains the service’s App Store number or, when Rinual doesn’t have one, the service’s English name from Rinual’s list.
  • For a subscription you named yourself, the search box starts with your subscription’s name, which you can change. Nothing is sent until you tap Search the App Store or the keyboard’s Search key.
  • Changing the country or region, or tapping Try Again, sends the request again.

Each request contains only the App Store number or the search term (at most 60 characters), the App Store country or region shown with it, and fixed settings that ask for apps only and, for a search, for at most 10 results. It contains no amounts, dates, notes, categories or anything about your other items. As with any internet request, Apple also receives your device’s network (IP) address and the standard details iOS adds to requests, such as the app’s name and version, the iOS version and your preferred languages. Rinual sends no cookies and keeps no web cache.

To show the apps found, Rinual then downloads their icons (at most 10) from Apple’s image servers (mzstatic.com), using the addresses in Apple’s answer. These icons are kept in memory only, while the screen is open. Only the icon you tap is saved: Rinual redraws it as a small 180 × 180-pixel image without metadata and saves it with that subscription. The app’s App Store number, name and address aren’t saved.

To suggest a country or region, Rinual uses, in this order: the one you last chose; your App Store account’s country, which iOS gives Rinual when a subscription’s add or edit screen shows the App Store icon option, and which isn’t sent to the developer; your iPhone’s region; or the United States. Your choice is remembered on this iPhone as soon as you change it. It isn’t synced, and Delete All Data removes it.

Apple handles these requests under its own privacy policy. App icons belong to their developers.

Photos

  • You can choose a photo as the icon of a subscription you named yourself (not one picked from Rinual’s list).
  • You choose it in the iOS photo picker, which runs outside Rinual. Rinual receives only the one image you pick. It doesn’t ask for access to your photo library and can’t see the rest of it. Rinual doesn’t use the camera.
  • If the photo is stored only in iCloud Photos, iOS may download it for the picker.
  • Rinual keeps only a small copy: the centre square of the image, redrawn as a 180 × 180-pixel JPEG of at most 150 KB, without location or other metadata. The original isn’t saved.
  • The icon is stored with the subscription on your iPhone. With iCloud sync on, it syncs as an icon image (see “How synced data is protected”). If the subscription appears in your widgets, a copy is kept for them.
  • Remove Icon, choosing another icon, deleting the subscription or Delete All Data removes it. With sync on, removing the icon or deleting the subscription also removes it from iCloud.

Notifications

  • Reminders are local notifications, scheduled by iOS on your iPhone. They aren’t sent through a server.
  • A reminder shows the item’s name and date, and the amount when Rinual knows it, for example for a subscription charge, a bill with an amount, or an installment. Depending on your iOS notification settings, iOS can show reminders on the Lock Screen.
  • Rinual asks for permission only after you save something with a reminder on and only after its own explanation (Turn On Reminders or Not Now), or when you tap Allow Notifications in Settings. It never asks when you first open it. A Not Now answer is remembered on this iPhone.
  • Mark as Paid works only while your iPhone is unlocked, and records the payment in Rinual. With sync on, that payment syncs like any other.
  • Remind Me Later opens Rinual and schedules one more local notification, on this iPhone only.
  • If a Mark as Paid tap can’t be recorded, Rinual keeps a short note on this iPhone (the time, an internal identifier, the due date and the reason, without names or amounts) until you dismiss its message or use Delete All Data.
  • With sync on, each item’s reminder settings and the default reminder time sync, and each of your iPhones that syncs and allows Rinual’s notifications schedules the same reminders itself.

Silent notifications from iCloud. When sync starts, Rinual registers your iPhone so iCloud can send it silent notifications when your data changes on another device. They come from iCloud, not from the developer. Rinual shows nothing for them and doesn’t read them; iCloud uses them to signal that there are changes to fetch, and they don’t carry the information you entered. Rinual doesn’t cancel this registration when you turn sync off.

Widgets

  • Rinual keeps a small summary for its widgets on your iPhone, whether or not you add a widget: the names, due dates, amounts, colours and icons of up to 8 upcoming items, and this month’s total and how much of it is paid.
  • The summary is kept in a storage area that only Rinual and its widgets share. Widgets read only that summary. They don’t open your data and don’t connect to the internet. The summary isn’t synced.
  • Names, due dates and icons aren’t marked as private, so the Lock Screen widget and widgets shown in StandBy can show them to anyone who sees your iPhone. Amounts, including the month’s total, are marked as private, so iOS can hide them when it hides private content, for example while your iPhone is locked. The bar that shows how much of the month is paid isn’t hidden.
  • The widget gallery shows sample data, not yours.
  • Tapping a widget opens Rinual using an internal identifier only.
  • Delete All Data removes the summary and its icons.

What Rinual connects to

Rinual makes internet connections only to Apple’s services:

  • iCloud, while sync is on: to sync your data and the three settings, and to check which account is signed in.
  • The App Store, each time Rinual starts: to ask whether the optional tip is offered and at what price, and to receive tips that finish later, such as one waiting for Ask to Buy approval. These requests carry nothing you entered.
  • The App Store’s search, only when you ask for an App Store icon (see “App Store icons”).

Links open outside Rinual, and only when you tap them. A link you saved for an item opens in your browser. Links in the About section of Settings, such as Privacy Policy and Send Feedback, open in your browser or your mail app. Rinual fetches nothing for these links, such as previews.

What Rinual doesn’t do

  • Rinual contains no advertising, analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code, and no third-party code.
  • It doesn’t track you across apps or websites, and doesn’t ask to.
  • It doesn’t send your information to the developer or to anyone else, apart from the requests to Apple described in this policy, and it doesn’t sell it.
  • The only permission it asks for is notifications, for reminders. It doesn’t ask for your location, contacts, calendars, camera, microphone or photo library.
  • It has no lock of its own and doesn’t use Face ID or Touch ID. iOS can require Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode to open Rinual; Rinual can’t turn that on or tell whether it’s on.
  • Its language, English or Arabic, is chosen in the iOS Settings app. Rinual stores no language choice of its own.

Deleting an item

  • Rinual asks before deleting an item. After you confirm, you can tap Undo for 5 seconds. If you leave Rinual during that time, the deletion is completed right away.
  • The deletion removes the item and everything that belongs to it: its details, payment history, amount changes, reminders and icon. With iCloud sync on, it’s also removed from iCloud and from your other iPhones.
  • Rinual keeps a small placeholder for each deleted item, so your other devices know it was deleted. The placeholder holds no name, notes, link, amounts or icon, only the item’s kind, status, currency code, dates and internal identifiers. It stays on your iPhone until you use Delete All Data and, with sync on, in iCloud until you use Delete from All Devices.
  • Deleting a category or billing source works the same way, without Undo, and its placeholder keeps no name. Items in a deleted category move to another category or to none; items that used a deleted billing source change to “Not selected”.

Delete All Data

Settings → Delete All Data deletes Rinual’s data after you type DELETE to confirm. While iCloud sync is working, you choose where:

  • Delete from All Devices deletes the data on this iPhone and in iCloud, placeholders included, and clears the three synced settings. It leaves a random identifier, with no content, in iCloud key-value storage, so your other iPhones know the deletion was intended and remove the data and their synced settings too. Sync stays on. The iCloud part finishes when this iPhone sends the change, so keep Rinual installed and open it while online until it says this iPhone has sent changes to iCloud.
  • Remove from This iPhone Only turns sync off, forgets which iCloud account this iPhone synced with, and removes the data from this iPhone. Nothing is deleted from iCloud or your other iPhones, and the synced settings stay as they are.

When sync is paused, or off after this iPhone has synced, only Remove from This iPhone Only is available. To delete the data in iCloud from Rinual, sync must be working with the account that holds it.

From this iPhone, Delete All Data removes everything you entered, Rinual’s settings and app states, its scheduled and delivered notifications, the widget summary and its icons, the copies kept for sync or recovery, and its internal records.

It doesn’t remove:

  • whether iCloud sync is on and, in some cases (for example after Delete from All Devices, which keeps sync on, or after Rinual restored its copy when the account changed), the identifier of an iCloud account it synced with;
  • a few internal sync markers, such as which deletion from all devices it last applied (yes-or-no values and random identifiers, with no content of yours);
  • if you used iCloud sync and didn’t choose Delete from All Devices, the three synced settings in iCloud key-value storage, of which iOS also keeps a copy on this iPhone;
  • iOS settings, such as notification permission and Rinual’s language;
  • copies you shared elsewhere, and backups of your iPhone made before.

It isn’t a secure erase of the iPhone’s storage.

Deleting the app

Deleting Rinual removes what it stored on this iPhone, including the widget summary. It doesn’t remove what is in iCloud (your private database and the synced settings), or earlier backups of your iPhone.

To delete your data from iCloud, use Delete All Data → Delete from All Devices while sync is working, before you delete the app. If you’ve already deleted it, you can install Rinual again, turn on iCloud sync with the same Apple Account, wait for your data to appear, and then use Delete from All Devices. You can also see which apps use your iCloud storage in the Settings app, under your name and then iCloud, where iOS may let you delete an app’s data. If you need help, contact the developer.

Optional tips

  • Rinual is free. You can leave an optional tip, an in-app purchase handled by Apple. A tip unlocks nothing. The tip option appears only when the App Store offers it.
  • Each time Rinual starts, it asks the App Store whether the tip is offered and at what price (see “What Rinual connects to”).
  • You pay in Apple’s own purchase sheet. Rinual never receives your payment details. It receives only Apple’s result (completed, waiting for approval, cancelled or failed). For a completed tip, it checks Apple’s signed transaction and tells the App Store the tip is finished.
  • Rinual keeps no record of tips, such as an amount, a date or a transaction, and syncs nothing about them.
  • Apple keeps the purchase in your Apple Account’s purchase history under its own terms and privacy policy, and handles refunds.
  • In TestFlight builds, Apple runs purchases in its test (sandbox) environment, where no actual payment is processed.

Diagnostics

  • Rinual writes short messages to iOS’s own system log on your iPhone, mostly when something goes wrong. A message names the operation and, for an error, an error code, never names, amounts, notes or anything else you entered. Rinual doesn’t send logs anywhere. They leave your iPhone only if you create and share a diagnostic report yourself, for example with Apple.
  • If you choose in iOS Settings to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give the developer crash reports and usage data for Rinual, under Apple’s terms. Rinual adds no crash reporting of its own.
  • If Rinual can’t finish deletions you confirmed, it shows a recovery screen and makes a copy of its deletion record in its temporary storage on your iPhone. The copy contains only internal identifiers, without names or amounts. It is shared only if you tap Share a Copy of the Record and choose where to send it. Delete All Data removes it, and iOS may also clear it.

TestFlight beta

If you test Rinual with Apple’s TestFlight app, Apple’s TestFlight service, not Rinual, collects beta information and gives some of it to the developer in App Store Connect, under Apple’s TestFlight terms and privacy policy:

  • crash reports, including how long Rinual ran before it crashed, and counts of installs, sessions and crashes;
  • your device model and its identifier, your iOS version and device language, and which build you installed;
  • feedback you choose to send, with its screenshots and comments, and, with feedback or a crash report you send, details such as your carrier, time zone, connection type, battery level, free storage, screen resolution, processor type and any paired Apple Watch;
  • how you were invited and whether you installed the beta. If you were invited by email, your name as the developer entered it and that email address. If you joined through a public link, Apple doesn’t show the developer your name or email address.

A screenshot can show your items’ names, amounts and icons, so check what it shows before you send it. The developer uses this information only to fix and improve Rinual.

Email

If you email the developer at nawafdev@icloud.com, for example with Send Feedback in Rinual’s Settings, which opens your own mail app, the developer receives your email address and whatever you write or attach. The developer uses it only to reply to you and to improve Rinual, and you can ask for your messages to be deleted. Rinual adds nothing to the message.

Children

Rinual is not directed at children. It has no accounts, asks no one for their age, and Rinual itself collects no information from anyone.

Security

  • Rinual’s data on your iPhone is protected by iOS’s data protection for app storage, which works with your iPhone’s passcode. Rinual adds no encryption of its own on the iPhone.
  • Rinual’s requests to the App Store’s search and image servers use HTTPS only.
  • Synced data is protected as described in “How synced data is protected”. For the strongest protection, Apple offers Advanced Data Protection for iCloud.
